    § Preface

    Atmosphere first; surface, second.

    Interior design begins where the architecture stops talking. The brief is rarely a list of finishes; it is a request for a particular weather, a tempo, a way of standing inside a room. My work begins by reading what a client cannot quite say, and ends when a space starts to behave the way they hoped it might. The eight projects collected here belong to three registers — hospitality, commercial, and cultural — and each one is, in its own way, an argument for restraint.

    The drawings are not afterthoughts. Plans, elevations and sections are the negotiation, not the documentation; they record decisions about adjacency and air, about what a guest's first hour should feel like, about whether a wall is structural or a piece of phrasing. I prefer materials that age; I prefer rooms that feel inevitable; I prefer the joinery to be quiet enough that the people in the room are louder.
